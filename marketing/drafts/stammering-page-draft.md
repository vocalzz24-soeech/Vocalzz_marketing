## Stammering Therapy for Children, Teens and Adults in Noida

Stammering (also called stuttering, or *haklana*) is when the flow of speech gets interrupted: repeating sounds,
stretching them, or getting stuck. It is common, it is nobody's fault, and with the right support many people
speak more easily and with more confidence. We see children, teens and adults at our Sector 137 clinic and online.

### Who it can help
- Young children (around 2–6 years) who have started repeating sounds or words, or getting stuck
- School children who avoid speaking in class or feel teased about their speech
- Teens and college students worried about presentations, vivas and interviews
- Working adults who stammer on the phone, in meetings or when introducing themselves
- Parents who want to know how to respond at home

### What we work on
- **Easier speech techniques:** for example gentle starts, light contacts and a comfortable speaking rate,
  practised step by step **[CHECK: techniques we actually use]**
- **Managing moments of stammering:** ways to move through a block with less tension
- **Confidence and less avoidance:** using the phone, ordering food, answering in class, interviews
- **For young children, parent-led practice:** short daily activities at home, guided by the therapist
  **[CHECK: do we use a parent-led programme such as Lidcombe? If not, say "parent-guided home practice"]**
- **Practice in real situations:** role-play of calls, introductions and presentations

### Should we wait or get an assessment?
Many young children go through a phase of repeating words while their language grows quickly, and some grow out of
it. It's a good idea to get an assessment if the stammering has lasted more than about 6 months, if your child
shows tension or struggle, avoids talking, gets upset about it, or if there is a family history of stammering.
For teens and adults, it is never too late to start. **[CHECK: wording of the "when to get assessed" signs]**

### Frequently asked questions

**Can stammering (haklana) be cured?**
There is no honest guarantee of a "cure". What therapy can do is help many children and adults speak more easily,
stammer with less struggle, and feel more confident speaking in daily life. Young children who get support early
often do well. We'll explain what to expect after the assessment.

**Is online stammering therapy possible?**
Yes. Many teens and adults practise well online, because the techniques are about how you speak, and practising
at home or at work is part of the therapy. Children usually need a parent to join.

**What should parents do when their child stammers?**
Stay calm and patient, keep eye contact, let your child finish without finishing words for them, and avoid "slow
down" or "take a breath". Speaking a little more slowly yourself helps. We'll give you specific home tips after
the assessment.
